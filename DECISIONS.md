## Research directions (not rulings; recorded for the owner, 2026-11-18)

| ID | Topic | Note |
|----|-------|------|
| X-01 | **In-body toxin sensor (the ISF signal, open problem #1).** | "Nanobots" are not a realistic path. The leading candidate is a **microneedle patch with electrochemical aptamer sensors**. Aptamers are short DNA or RNA strands chosen to bind one molecule, and on an electrode they give a continuous reading. They have been run continuously in animals and in early microneedle work in people for other molecules. **Nanobodies** (very small camelid antibodies, already studied for next-generation antivenom) are an alternative binder. Open questions: binders that work across a whole toxin family, 12 h+ sensor life, and whether venom reaches skin fluid early enough to give warning. Builder's general knowledge, not checked against current literature (R-13: no project details in web searches). |
| X-02 | **What the responder carries.** | Antivenom is normally given by IV infusion with anaphylaxis cover, not by a field auto-injector. Field-usable small-molecule inhibitors (e.g. varespladib for PLA2, marimastat for SVMP) are investigational and fit the "family-matched payload" idea. Everything here stays trainer-only. The checklist content is for a toxinologist. |
